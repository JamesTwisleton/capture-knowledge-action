Capture → Knowledge → Action

Turn your knowledge into action automatically, with the tools you already know.

CKA is an open-source framework that listens to your meetings and documents, files what matters into your knowledge base, and updates your tickets — using whichever meeting tool, wiki, work tracker and AI your team already has. Nothing changes state without a human saying so, out loud and on purpose.

Amanda, the team lead, reviews the proposal. One click — ticket closed, fully audited. And because the AI has got every closure right for two weeks, CKA offers to handle closures on its own from here. Her call, reversible any time. Don't just trust it — see it earn it.

The problem

You can buy this today. The catch: the whole suite. #

Meetings-to-knowledge-to-action automation is already on sale — but only inside a single vendor's walls. Atlassian will sell it to you across Jira and Confluence, Google across Meet and Workspace, Microsoft across Teams and 365. Each works well, right up to the edge of its own ecosystem — and at its own markup.

The usual way

Pay one vendor for their bundle — their meeting AI, their wiki, their tracker, upsold module by module. It only works if every tool you use is theirs. A stack that spans providers, or wants to swap one piece out, isn't supported; it's a reason to re-buy the whole suite.

The CKA way

Composable. Every piece — meeting capture, knowledge store, work tracker, AI model — is a swappable provider. Mix one vendor's tracker with an offline wiki and a local model; swap any piece later without touching the rest. Open source, so nobody marks up the glue.

⚠ DATA SENSITIVITY — READ BEFORE YOU SET ANYTHING UP #

This product sends meeting transcripts and documents to whichever AI providers you configure. Transcripts routinely contain names, project details, commercial information and occasionally conversations about people and personnel matters.

Before enabling any cloud AI provider, confirm that your organisation permits that data to be sent to that provider. The expected setup is your organisation's own contracted model. Where that isn't available, local self-hosted models are a first-class option and keep every byte of data inside your own infrastructure.
