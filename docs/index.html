The problem

Everyone is building the same pipeline. Badly.

Meeting happens → someone tries to summarise it → maybe it reaches your wiki → maybe someone remembers to update the ticket. Every organisation builds this by hand, wired to one meeting tool, one AI, one wiki, one tracker — and rebuilds it from scratch the moment any of those change.

The usual way

A bespoke pipeline bolted to Confluence, or Jira, or one specific AI vendor. Works great — until your organisation uses something else, or wants to switch providers, or needs to keep data off a particular cloud. Then it's a rewrite, not a config change.

The CKA way

Every piece — meeting capture, knowledge store, work tracker, AI model — is a swappable provider. Change your mind about which AI you use, or which wiki, or whether it runs in the cloud at all, and nothing else in the system has to change.

Meeting → knowledge → action, automatically

Three stages, connected by events — each one usable on its own, or chained end to end.

1 · Capture

The meeting happens

3 · Action

Work items update themselves

Items mentioned get a comment automatically. Say the word — literally, your team's trigger phrase — and a real update gets proposed.

Review, then trust
Don't just trust it — see it earn it.
  • Nothing that changes state happens without your team's trigger phrase, spoken on purpose.
  • Every proposal shows its confidence and exactly which AI model made it.
  • You approve or reject. CKA tracks its own accuracy against your decisions — per action type.
  • Earn a good track record, and it offers to act on its own. Keep getting it wrong, and it offers to switch itself off instead.

⚠ DATA SENSITIVITY — READ BEFORE YOU SET ANYTHING UP

This product sends meeting transcripts and documents to whichever AI providers you configure. Transcripts routinely contain names, project details, commercial information and occasionally conversations about people and personnel matters.

Before enabling any cloud AI provider, confirm that your organisation permits that data to be sent to that provider. The expected setup is your organisation's own contracted model. Where that isn't available, local self-hosted models are a first-class option and keep every byte of data inside your own infrastructure.
